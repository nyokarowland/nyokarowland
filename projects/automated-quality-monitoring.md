# Customer-Service Call Monitoring and Scoring Validation

**Project type:** Professional team project  
**Focus:** Quality assurance, call monitoring, scoring validation, and process improvement

## Overview

I worked with a team to develop and validate an automated quality-monitoring process for customer-service calls. The process evaluated calls against defined call-handling standards and keyword criteria. My contribution included conducting test calls, checking whether the resulting scores reflected what happened in the conversation, identifying missed results, and helping refine the evaluation logic.

The examples below illustrate the call-flow areas that a quality-monitoring process can evaluate. They are generic examples, not a company script or a copy of production scoring rules.

## Call-Monitoring Areas

| Call stage | What to review | Illustrative example |
|---|---|---|
| Opening and greeting | Did the representative welcome the caller, identify the company and themself, and offer help? | “Thank you for calling [Company]. My name is [Name]. How may I help you?” |
| Acknowledging the concern | Did the representative listen and show that they understood the reason for the call? | “I understand you are calling about [concern]. Let me look into that.” |
| Empathy and apology | Did the representative respond appropriately to the customer's experience? | “I’m sorry this happened. I understand how frustrating that can be.” |
| Addressing the issue | Did the representative investigate, explain the next steps, and take appropriate action? | “Here is what I found and what I can do to help.” |
| Confirming resolution | Did the representative check that the concern was resolved or explain any remaining follow-up? | “Has this addressed your concern?” |
| Closing the call | Did the representative offer further help and thank the caller while identifying the company? | “Is there anything else I can help you with? Thank you for calling [Company].” |

## How I Helped Validate the Scoring

- Conducted test calls that included different parts of the customer-service call flow.
- Compared the automated result with what was actually said and done during the call.
- Identified missed results where a required behavior occurred but was not recognized.
- Helped refine the evaluation logic so the scoring better reflected the call-handling standards.

An automated keyword check can miss a valid response phrased differently or match a phrase without the intended behavior. Test calls and human review helped evaluate whether the scoring accurately represented the customer interaction.
